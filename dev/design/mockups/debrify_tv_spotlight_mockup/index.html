Debrify TV, in the Spotlight idiom

Debrify TV is the app's best idea and its oldest-looking screen: a wall of identical red CH n tiles that tells you nothing about a channel until you press it and find out whether it plays. This redraws it in the same language as the new onboarding and settings — navy ground, mono eyebrows, borderless lift focus — and turns the channel list into a rail and a stage, where the stage answers the one question the grid never could: what is actually on this channel right now.

03What nothing on this page may promise

An earlier draft of this mock called the strip “Up next on this channel”. That was a lie, and the code says so in four places. Debrify TV does not have a running order, and three of the rules that decide what actually plays cannot be evaluated until the provider has been asked.

ClaimWhy it can't be made
“This plays next”_selectTorrentsForPlayback ends in list.shuffle(Random()) (:1525). The order is redrawn on every tune. There is no next.
“This will play at all”Whether the provider has a given infohash cached is only known when it answers. An uncached pick is skipped and the queue moves on.
“This is in your size range”Size is a per-file rule, and Real-Debrid “hands back a flat list of links with no per-file metadata, so a file's size is only knowable AFTER unrestricting it” (_rdLinkPassesSizeRules). The pool row's size is the whole torrent.
“Your filters will be respected”Both degrade on purpose. A quality filter matching zero returns the unfiltered pool (:1428); after _rdSizeRejectionLimit consecutive rejections the size filter is relaxed for the rest of the session (:1505). Both are correct behaviour — an unplayable channel is worse — but neither can be promised in advance.

So the strip is inventory, not schedule: “What's in this channel”, captioned a sample of the pool · nothing here is a running order. It answers the question you actually have when twelve channels are named things you half-remember — what kind of stuff is in this one — and it answers it with real rows from real cache.

And where a promise can't be made, offer a choice instead. OK on a plate plays that title. It is the one behaviour on this page that is not already implemented, and it is cheap: _watchWithCachedTorrents already takes a list, so a single-element list is the whole change. That turns the strip from a claim about the future into a control over it.

What survives the correction. At your quality stays a first-class number, because that one filter genuinely is applied to the cache before the shuffle — it is the same function call, on the same rows. The relax-fallback snackbar is currently the only way to learn your filter emptied a channel, and it fires after you pressed Play and waited. That discovery still moves to before the press. Size simply isn't part of the claim.

04The focus rule

Two behaviours, chosen by what the thing is — never mixed, and never a coloured ring.

TEXT ROWS

Invert and lift

Rail rows, setting cards, buttons and chips flood white, take dark ink, rise ~5px and scale ~1.02, with a soft specular sweep across the fill. This is the settings and onboarding mechanic, unchanged.

ART PLATES

Lift only

Sample plates never invert — inverting a card whose subject is an image destroys the image. They scale 1.045, rise 10px, take a white hairline and a large soft shadow. Straight from APPLE_LOOK_PLAN.md §2.1.

NOWHERE

No accent ring

The crimson stays on badges, pips, the tuning bar and the value pills. It is never the cursor. The shipped card draws a 3px accent border today; it goes.

05Artwork is a layer, and it is optional

Flip Art on above. The plates keep their geometry and swap their beds for stills. Everything else on the page is identical, because artwork is the only part of this design that costs anything to add.

  • Art off is the shippable state. Every number and every title on the stage comes out of SQLite. No request leaves the device, so it works offline, on a cold boot, and on a TV box with no headroom.
  • Art on needs a cache, not a lookup. MovieMetadataService.lookupFromFilename() already turns a release name into Cinemeta metadata — but calling it on focus change would hit the network on every D-pad press. It needs a persistent infohash → {poster, backdrop, title, year, runtime} table, filled by the same background prefetch that already warms the pool.
  • Some titles will never resolve. Debrify channels are keyword channels; plenty of pool entries are scene names a metadata service has never seen. The typographic bed is not a placeholder for missing art — it is the design for a plate that has none, which is why it carries the raw release name and the matched keyword.
  • Do not put art in the rail. A channel is six keywords, not one title; a mosaic of three posters says less than the numbers do and costs three lookups per row.

08Deliberately not drawn

  • A programme guide. Debrify TV picks at random from a pool; there is no schedule to show and inventing one would be a lie the player immediately contradicts.
  • Poster mosaics on channel rows. §5.
  • Per-channel provider or filter overrides. The data model has no column for either, and adding one is a feature decision rather than a layout one.
  • A "recently played" row. Nothing records what a channel has already played — the pick is random and stateless by design.
  • Channel reordering. channel_number is a UNIQUE column assigned at insert; making it draggable means renumbering inside a transaction, which is real work and not part of a repaint.
