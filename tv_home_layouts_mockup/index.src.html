Home screen · five concepts

Five ways home

Canvas is the shipping default — full-bleed art, one shelf, rail tabs. These five are alternatives that sit beside it in Settings → Home Layout, not replacements. Every one is drawn at true TV scale (1920×1080, which is 2× the app's logical pixels), uses the same 64px ghost rail, and is built only from parts the app already has: the underlay trailer engine, the poster cells, the rails, the logo-first identity block.

They're live. Click a screen, then drive it with the arrow keys — the focus model is the design. A layout that can't be navigated on a remote isn't a layout.

Smoothness budgetTransform and opacity only. No blur, no full-screen gradient tweens, no saveLayer over the video hole — the rules the current home already lives by.
What can moveCard rise, rail glide, art cross-dissolve, identity fade. Everything else snaps.
What each costsCalled out per concept, in the same terms as the perf playbook: repaint area, image decodes, and whether the trailer region changes shape.
I

Atrium

Vertical split · dossier + wall

The screen breaks the way a magazine spread breaks: type on one side, picture on the other.

Live · arrows

←→ along a row · ↑↓ between the two rows

Shape

A hard vertical cut at 38%. Left is a flat ink column that never takes focus — logo, IMDb, meta, four lines of synopsis, and the key hints. Right is art, full height, with a two-row poster wall sitting on its lower half.

Motion

Focus only ever lives in the wall. The art cross-dissolves at 260ms; the dossier re-sets — logo swaps, meta and synopsis fade at 180ms. The seam gets one feather so the cut reads as deliberate.

Cost

The left third is a ColoredBox: no gradient, no tween, zero repaint while you scrub. The trailer region is a fixed rect that never changes shape, so start/stop has nothing to animate.

Cheapest identity blockReuses: boxed _HeroTrailerLayer, _ArtPoster, railsTwo rows = shorter art
II

Mosaic

Grid · no hero

For the night you don't want to be told what to watch — you want to see everything you own.

Live · arrows

←→ across · ↑↓ down the wall — the header is the only identity

Shape

No hero at all. Seven posters across, rows running off the bottom edge, on a heavily darkened wash of whatever has focus. Identity moves to a fixed top band; category tabs sit opposite it.

Motion

The card rises 1.055 with a white ring — the existing _CardFocusRise, untouched. The wash snaps rather than tweens, the house rule for full-screen layers. Rows glide at 140ms.

Cost

The lightest of the five: one image, one flat veil, one GridView. No trailer engine on the board — an optional 2s-dwell trailer can drop into the top-right inset later.

Lowest GPU costBest for many catalogsBrowses, doesn't sit back
III

Promenade

Centre-locked strip

One title, dead centre, always. The strip travels — the focus never does.

Live · arrows

←→ pull the strip through the centre · ↑↓ change rail

Shape

Full-bleed art like Canvas, but the composition is symmetric: logo and meta centred in the lower third, and a strip of 16:9 stills below where the focused card is pinned to the middle of the screen.

Motion

Neighbours rest at 0.86 scale and 55% opacity, so the centre reads as lit. Moving is one transform on the track. Rail changes announce themselves in a centred eyebrow with chevrons.

Cost

Free, essentially: rails already scroll with alignment: 0.5, so centre-lock is the behaviour we already ship — this concept just commits to it visually. Track moves by transform; no layout pass.

Calmest of the fiveReuses Canvas wholesaleWants 16:9 stills; falls back to posters
IV

Deck

Object on a stage

The trailer stops being the wallpaper and becomes a thing sitting on the table.

Live · arrows

←→ deal the next card forward

Shape

Deep ink ground. The hero is a rounded 16:9 card floating right of centre with the next two titles stacked behind it; identity stands to its left; one compact rail runs along the bottom.

Motion

Moving right deals: the stack slides left and the next card lands in the slot at 260ms, transform and opacity only. The peek cards are static art — never a second video.

Cost

The punch hole follows the card's laid-out rect, which Canvas already proved needs no native work. Rounded corners are four ink wedges painted above the hole — plain paint over the hole is the proven pattern; only Opacity is banned.

Boldest departureReuses _CanvasIdentity unchangedOne new corner painter
V

Tonight

Resume-first dashboard

It answers "what do I press play on" before you've finished asking.

Live · arrows

→ into Up Next · ↑↓ through the queue · ↓ to the rail

Shape

Three zones. A large 16:9 resume card carrying the show logo, episode, progress and time left; a vertical Up Next queue beside it; one rail underneath for everything that isn't already started.

Motion

Crossing zones is a 140ms glide. Taking focus in the queue swaps the big card's art — the only image change on screen, so the frame stays quiet while you read.

Cost

No full-bleed art anywhere: a flat ground and one image rect. After Mosaic, the lightest thing here — and the only one whose cost doesn't grow with catalogue size.

Most useful dailyReuses CW rows + boxed trailerNeeds a real Continue Watching list

Which one, and why

All five are additive: a new value in tv_home_style, a new branch in the board build, nothing removed. Canvas stays the default until you say otherwise.

ConceptReads asTrailer regionNew codeBest when
I AtriumEditorial, composedFixed right rectLow — split scaffold + vertical identityYou want the synopsis to matter again
II MosaicA well-run storeNone (optional inset)Low — grid board, no heroMany catalogs, you know what you want
III PromenadeCinema marqueeFull-bleed, as todayLowest — Canvas with a centred stripYou liked Canvas and want it calmer
IV DeckPremium hardwareCard rect (follows widget)Medium — deal animation + corner painterYou want the boldest visual change
V TonightA conciergeCard rect, optionalMedium — 2D zones, vertical queueYou mostly resume, rarely browse

My pick to build first: III Promenade, then II Mosaic. Promenade is nearly free — it inherits Canvas's whole engine and its centre-lock is behaviour we already ship — so it lands as a genuinely different feel for very little risk. Mosaic is the one that complements rather than competes: it's the browse mode Canvas can't be, and it's the cheapest thing on the list to run on the Mi Box. Atrium is the one I'd build if you want the most obviously "designed" screen, and Deck is the showpiece — most expensive, most likely to be the screenshot people share.
