The bottom dock, re-measured

Four directions for the touch/desktop control dock — the widget in controls.dart that every non-TV platform gets. Televisions keep TvControls and are out of scope. Each concept carries the full button inventory the player can throw at it, and every one is drawn on the density scale in §3, which is the part that answers “the menu looks small on high-pixel devices”.

Palette
Re-skins all four concepts live. The player’s own setting — it does not follow the app theme.

02 — Four directions

Concepts

All four are drawn at density k = 1.0 (the phone baseline) so they compare fairly. §3 scales them. Every concept keeps the complete inventory — nothing is dropped, only re-ranked.

C1

Two-Tier Dock

Split the one row in two. A centred transport tier — prev · −10 · play · +10 · next — sits directly above the scrubber at full size and never moves. Everything else drops to a tools tier that wraps on wide viewports instead of scrolling. Same buttons, same code paths; only arrangement and size change.

03 — The density scale

Fixing “it looks small on high-pixel devices”

The instinct is to bump the font size. That’s the wrong lever twice over: the app already clamps textScaler at 1.3, and text is the one thing that does scale — icons, padding and hit targets don’t. What the dock needs is a single density factor derived from the viewport, applied to every metric at once.

Drive it off shortest side in logical pixels, not DPR. Flutter has already divided physical pixels by DPR, so DPR alone tells you nothing — what matters is how much logical canvas the dock has to hold its own against. The one exception is desktop at 100% OS scaling, where DPR collapses to 1.0 and logical size explodes; that gets a nudge.

Two refinements worth building in from the start:

  • The unscaled-desktop nudgeIf devicePixelRatio ≤ 1.25 and shortest side ≥ 1080, the OS is doing no scaling at all and one logical px is one hardware px — multiply k by a further 1.15. This is the exact case behind the complaint.
  • A user override, not just an inferenceShip Settings → Player → Control size: Auto · Small · Medium · Large mapping to k×0.85 / k / k×1.2 / k×1.45. Viewing distance is something no MediaQuery can measure, and this closes the argument permanently.
  • Don’t double-apply text scaleThe label already rides the platform’s clamped textScaler. Feed labels --lb as the base size and let Flutter scale it, rather than multiplying twice and blowing out the row on accessibility settings.
  • TV is already handledisTelevision routes to TvControls, and tvOS gets the 2× logical-canvas correction in main.dart:568. The ladder is phone and desktop only — don’t let it reach the TV path.
04 — Palette, as a player setting

Four colours, and they belong to the player

Not tied to the app theme. The dock lives over footage whose colour grade changes every second, which is a different problem from skinning a settings page — so it gets its own setting, and the app theme is free to move without dragging the player with it.

Hue is the smaller half of “premium”. A saturated colour poured into a flat circle still reads as a coloured div. What the streaming apps you’re thinking of actually do is treat the primary as a lit object — a hot light source in the top-left of the fill, a specular hairline along the upper edge, and a glow it casts down onto the scrim. Every palette below ships that treatment; swap between them and watch the play button, not just the colour.

default		 #FF3DA6 → #6D2BFF Hot magenta falling into deep violet — the modern premium-streaming gradient, and the only one that still reads as Debrify rather than as somebody else. The widest hue travel of the four; needs the gradient angle held at 147° or it bands.
Crimson#FF3A52 → #B00418 Cinema red done properly: a hot scarlet dropping to oxblood, not the flat single-value red that ships today. Highest urgency, reads instantly over any grade. Competes with the record red. On a live channel, hold the two apart deliberately.
Aurum#FFE7A3 → #C1861A Metallic gold — champagne into old brass. The prestige register: awards, marquees, anything that wants to feel expensive rather than energetic. Only palette needing dark ink on the primary. Goes brassy if the light end dulls.
Ice#7BF1FF → #0A5CFF Electric cyan into deep blue. The coldest and most technical — highest contrast over dark footage of the four. Blue is the default chrome colour of half the industry; the least distinctive here.

One colour stays fixed in all four: the record red #F43F5E. It is semantic, not decorative — a live capture is a state the user must recognise instantly, and a palette that could recolour it would be a palette that could hide it. Same rule applies to any future error or warning state in the dock.

05 — Recommendation

Ship the scale first, then C1 + C2 as one widget

  1. Density ladder alone, as its own change. It needs no redesign and no new sheets — a DockMetrics.of(context) plus replacing the constants in netflix_control_button.dart. That fixes the reported complaint, and fixes the sub-minimum hit targets everywhere as a side effect. Lowest risk in the document.
  2. Then C1 as the structure. Transport gets its own centred tier at 1.3× target size so Play/Pause stops drifting; tools drop below. Same buttons, same callbacks — a layout change, not a behaviour change.
  3. Take C2’s overflow sheet as C1’s narrow-width behaviour. Below roughly 700lp of width, the tools tier keeps three contextual chips and a More; above it, the tier wraps and shows everything. One widget, one breakpoint — and the sheet is where the duplicate “Guide” and the meaningless desktop “Rotate” get resolved by construction.
  4. Fold C4 in as the ≥1080lp arrangement of that same widget: zones instead of a centred stack, icon-only with tooltips, keyboard hints, edge-to-edge scrubber with hover preview. Three layouts of one widget beats three widgets.
  5. Hold C3. The thumb rail is the most interesting idea here and the only genuinely new one, but it fights the IPTV info panel and the zap banner for the right edge, and both already ship. Worth prototyping after the above lands — not worth blocking on.
  6. Retire the Netflix red in the same pass. It is currently in three places — primary chip, seek track and seek thumb — and all three become the palette accent, defaulting to Ultraviolet. Ship the four palettes from §4 as Settings → Player → Control colour at the same time; the tokens cost nothing once the constants are being touched anyway, and the record red stays put.

Mocks are HTML approximations, not Flutter — dock metrics are token-driven and match the proposed ladder exactly, but easing, blur and font rendering will differ. Source audited on branch tvos_port. TvControls and the native Android TV player are out of scope.
