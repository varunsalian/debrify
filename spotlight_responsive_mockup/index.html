Spotlight & Showcase on phone, tablet and desktop

The TV design, carried to every other form factor — same language, not the same layout. Tablet and desktop keep the TV composition (left identity, proportional shelves) and add pointer affordances. The phone gets the Apple-phone idiom: centered identity stack, bigger cards, captions below the art, an integrated episode card, and a season dropdown. Search survives as a top button that unfolds into today's field + Catalog/Keyword toggle.

Everything below is live: tap the search button, tap the season pill, tap the hero dots (the hero also advances itself every 6 seconds), hover the cards on the desktop frame. All frames scroll.

01 · MOBILE — 390 × 844 LOGICALPhone, portrait

Home leads with the hero — the search field no longer eats the top of the page; it lives behind the magnifier and unfolds over the hero. Cards grow from 13.5% of the width (the TV fraction) to 24%, matching both the Apple TV phone app and, incidentally, our own classic phone board. The detail page keeps Showcase's band structure but re-cuts every cell: episodes become one integrated card at 62% width — still, eyebrow, title, synopsis, runtime, kebab — and the season rail becomes a pill + anchored popup.

The hero art on the detail page is the backdrop, cover-cropped. Apple uses purpose-made portrait key art there; catalogs don't give us that, so the crop is the honest fallback — the scrim carries legibility regardless of where the crop lands. If a portrait-art source ever exists (some addons expose poster art without baked titles), the slot upgrades for free.

02 · TABLET — 1194 × 834 LOGICAL, LANDSCAPETablet

The TV layout survives almost verbatim — left-aligned identity, full-bleed hero, shelves riding over its lower edge. Two touch adaptations: the poster fraction gets a bump (13.5% → 16%) because fingers need targets a remote never did, and every episode card carries a visible kebab instead of hold-OK. The season chips stay chips — there's room.

03 · DESKTOP — 1440 × 810 LOGICALDesktop

The TV proportions verbatim — this is the panel the design was drawn for, minus ten feet. Pointer replaces DPAD: cards lift on hover exactly as they lift on focus, the hero pages from the dots, and the search button unfolds a centered bar with the same field + toggle. Wheel scrolls; nothing needs a keyboard.
